What if the biggest barrier to free healthcare wasn't distance or money—but information reaching the right people?

A month ago, our team participated in NEPATHONE VOL.1, a 48-hour hackathon in Tilottama, Nepal. We didn't win. But we discovered something more important: a real problem affecting thousands of people in underserved communities.

The Problem:
In Nepal, government agencies, INGOs, and NGOs organize hundreds of free health camps annually—dental care, eye checkups, vaccinations, general screenings. These services are completely free and critically needed. But information is scattered across municipality websites, health post boards, Facebook groups, and word-of-mouth. No centralized system exists.

The people who need these services most—rural communities, low-income families, elderly citizens—often miss out simply because the information never reaches them.

Our Solution: MedFree
We're building a centralized data system that collects health camp information from municipalities and health posts across Nepal, then distributes it through multiple channels to ensure NO ONE gets left behind.

Here's how we reach everyone:

For digitally connected users:
• Website with location-based search and filtering (https://medfree.adarashagaihre.com.np)
• Push notifications and email alerts
• WhatsApp and Viber messages

For communities with limited internet:
• SMS notifications to mobile numbers in targeted areas
• Automated voice calls with camp details
• Smart opt-out: Users who register online won't get redundant SMS/calls

For areas with no network coverage:
• Vehicle-based announcements with loudspeakers (e-rickshaws, motorcycles)
• Community miking reaching every household
• Word-of-mouth amplification

When a dental camp is organized in Area XYZ, we don't advertise our website—we advertise the camp itself. Everyone in that area gets the message: "ABC Dental Camp on [date] at [location]. Organized by [org]. For details: medfree.com"

Why This Matters:
Our target communities often lack internet access, smartphones, or digital literacy. But everyone has a basic phone and SIM card. By combining digital and traditional channels, we ensure information reaches everyone—from tech-savvy youth to elderly citizens who've never used the internet.

The Core System:
The heart of MedFree isn't the website—it's the centralized database and multi-channel distribution system. The website is just one way to access the information. The database is public and open-source, so anyone can build on top of it.

What We Learned:
The hackathon taught us to build fast. But what came after taught us to build for equity. Real social impact means meeting people where they are—whether that's online, on their basic phone, or through a loudspeaker in their village.

This is social work, not a business. Everything is free and open.
